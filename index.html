Giovanni Briglia
PhD student, National PhD in AI · UNIMORE · visiting the University of Oxford

Agents that learn modular models of what their actions do.

I study how agents can learn structured world models: factored models in which each variable has its own local mechanism on an explicit graph, and how to use them to decide under uncertainty. With Neural Bayesian Networks, the graph stays explicit while each conditional is learned by a neural density estimator, so models run efficiently on GPUs, keep a modular, inspectable structure, and can be revised one mechanism at a time when the world changes. At Oxford I am exploring how such models connect to Markov decision processes and formal methods, to reason about how far an agent's decisions can be trusted.

Supervised by Franco Zambonelli and Stefano Mariani at UNIMORE. At Oxford I’m hosted by Alessandro Abate and Francesco Fabiano.

Research agenda

Three questions shape my PhD

They go from how a world model is represented, to how decisions built on it can be trusted, to what changes when many agents share one environment.

Representation

Given factored state variables and a causal graph, what model of transition mechanisms can an agent learn from its own actions, revise locally when only some mechanisms change, and reuse across environments, tasks and policies?

Related papers
arXiv 2026 · Neural Bayesian Networks NeurIPS-W 2026 · Continual world models PRIMA 2024 · Causal models for RL agents
Decision-making

How should such models enter planning, policy optimisation and value estimation? And, in ongoing work at Oxford, how can the uncertainty of a learned model be carried into the decisions made with it, so that we can say how far those decisions can be trusted?

Related papers
PerCom-W 2026 · Causal value estimation AREA @ ECAI 2025 · Safe multi-robot policies
Multi-agent

How can causality-driven RL extend to multi-agent settings, where cooperation and competition add new layers of interdependence?

Related papers
arXiv 2025 · A roadmap for causal MARL CLaRAMAS 2026 · Goals, issues and taxonomy AAMAS 2026 · Epistemic planning with GNNs
